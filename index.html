[ 01 ] Origin

I'm from São Gabriel, a town of 60,000 in the far south of Brazil, and the fifth generation of a family that farms rice and cattle there.

[ 02 ] Self-taught

I taught myself English, AI and audience growth, mostly from YouTube. Most of what's on this page happened after I moved to Porto Alegre on my own, in the middle of a school year, because that was the only window I had.

Sincra DoctorsHospitalsForecastSecurity Fig. 02 · Now building · 2026–
Tech co-founder · one of three

Doctors shouldn't pay interest on money that's already theirs.

In Brazil, a surgeon can wait months to be paid for an operation, and the hospital often can't tell where that money is stuck: internal audit, the insurer, a missing signature. Sincra rebuilds every procedure's path from surgery to payout and predicts how much each doctor will receive, when, and with what confidence.

Fig. 04Training runAI · ONIA · 2024–2025

Gold in Brazil's first AI Olympiad.

One of 28 gold medals among more than 716,000 participants. Then one missing line of code between me and the international team.

The first edition of ONIA, Brazil's National Artificial Intelligence Olympiad, which selects the national team for the International Olympiad in AI. It went from theory and ethics, to building regressions in Python, to a final round where I built a computer-vision algorithm for competing robots, trained on Santos Dumont II, the national supercomputer at LNCC.

Afterwards I was named an ambassador for the second edition and brought classmates into it.

716K+participants
28gold medals
1of them mine

The one that got away. The selection for the international team ran training on jobs capped at 20 minutes. My script saved a checkpoint every epoch but never loaded one, so every job started from zero, and my model stalled around 70% accuracy when it could have reached about 95%. One missing line of code between me and Beijing. I still think about it.

def train(data_yaml, epochs, batch_size, img_size):
    # YOLOv8n (nano) para rapidez em 20 minutos
    last = 'runs/detect/exp/weights/last.pt'
    if os.path.exists(last):
        return YOLO(last).train(resume=True)
    model = YOLO('yolov8n.yaml')
    model.train(
        data=data_yaml,
        epochs=epochs,
        batch=batch_size,
        imgsz=img_size,
        workers=4,
        name='exp',
        save_period=1,    # checkpoint cada época

    )

When I joined InovaTec, my high school's student-run innovation club, I found a lot of ambition and almost no one who could build. The club was trying to make the platform in Bubble, with poor results. As head of technology I took over the build, rebuilt it from scratch with Lovable when AI coding was brand new, and we launched in about four months.

It became a partner of Rede Atados, Brazil's largest volunteering network, and was covered by RBS TV, SBT, Zero Hora and Brasil de Fato. Before graduating I trained the students who took over. In July 2026 they used it to raise part of the money to renovate an art school in Morro da Cruz, a low-income community in Porto Alegre.

Through the club I also ran AI workshops for 50 low-income students and AI talks for 40 teachers.

Fig. 06AudienceInstagram · 2023–2024

A crab, a cat, 316,000 followers. All sold.

I noticed my feed kept serving the same repeatable video formats with millions of views, so I tried to reverse-engineer them.

Followers at the time of sale. The screenshots are from earlier, on the way up.

The first two pages went nowhere. Then a crab took off: @seusiriqueijo, “the most famous crab in Brazil.” A cat drinking water did it again on @gatinhoajude, where one reel reached 340,000 accounts. Over one 30-day window, a page reached 7.2 million accounts.

I couldn't monetize them (I had no idea how to build a sales funnel), so I sold all three: for R$1,200, R$800 and R$160. Along the way I placed top five in a clipping competition, closed a paying mentorship client who found me through my DMs, and learned the hard way to sell before building. I still help friends grow their audiences.

    Small talk

    The short answers are here. For the long ones, ask your AI at the bottom: it reads my notes first.

    Where are you from?

    São Gabriel, a town of 60,000 in the far south of Brazil, where my family has farmed rice and cattle for five generations. I moved to Porto Alegre on my own in 2024, in the middle of a school year, and I'm in São Paulo now.

    How did you learn all of this?

    Mostly YouTube: English (C2 by 16), AI, audience growth, and rockets, where BPS.space did most of the teaching.

    What is Sincra, in one breath?

    A fintech for medical receivables. It reads what a hospital already has, predicts how much each doctor will receive and when, and lets doctors and hospitals advance that money through partner funds, with the cost shown first. I'm the tech co-founder, one of three.

    Which miss do you think about most?

    The AI Olympiad selection. Training ran in 20-minute jobs and I never resumed from a checkpoint, so every job started from zero. My model stalled around 70% when it could have reached about 95%. One missing line between me and Beijing.

    Why sell the Instagram pages?

    I had the audience but no idea how to build a sales funnel, so it wasn't turning into anything. Selling all three taught me to sell before building.
