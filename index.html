Now building

Sincra

Fintech · Healthcare

Role
Tech co-founder, one of three
Since
2026
Site
sincra.app ↗

In Brazil, a surgeon can wait months to be paid for an operation, and the hospital often can't tell where that money is stuck: internal audit, the insurer, a missing signature. Doctors end up paying bank interest on money that is already theirs.

Sincra reads what a hospital already has (its management system, the insurers' TISS returns and the bank statement), rebuilds every procedure's path from surgery to payout, and predicts how much each doctor will receive, when, and with what confidence. Doctors and hospitals can then advance those receivables through partner funds, seeing the cost before they confirm.

My part

I lead product and engineering. I built the product prototype (the doctor, hospital and admin views) and the site.

Where it is

We started as a shift-scheduling tool for hospitals, changed the thesis during Link's Startup School, and are now structuring our first operations with hospitals and funding partners.

Voluntaria+

When
2024–2025
Role
Head of technology, InovaTec
Team
4 students, led by me

A platform that connects volunteers with NGOs across Rio Grande do Sul. When I joined InovaTec, my high school's student-run innovation club, I found a lot of ambition and almost no one who could build. The club was trying to make the platform in Bubble, with poor results. I took over the build, rebuilt it from scratch with Lovable when AI coding was brand new, and we launched in about four months.

170+volunteers connected
20regional NGOs
80+donors at the school's largest blood drive
R$6,300raised so the Union of the Blind of RS could set up its Braille printer

It became a partner of Rede Atados, Brazil's largest volunteering network, and was covered by RBS TV, SBT, Zero Hora and Brasil de Fato. Before graduating I trained the students who took over. In July 2026 they used it to raise part of the money to renovate an art school in Morro da Cruz, a low-income community in Porto Alegre.

Through the club I also ran AI workshops for 50 low-income students and AI talks for 40 teachers.

AI · Competition

Gold, 1st National AI Olympiad

When
2024–2025
Field
716,000+ participants
Result
1 of 28 gold medals

The first edition of ONIA, Brazil's National Artificial Intelligence Olympiad, which selects the national team for the International Olympiad in AI. It went from theory and ethics, to building regressions in Python, to a final round where I built a computer-vision algorithm for competing robots, trained on Santos Dumont II, the national supercomputer at LNCC.

Afterwards I was named an ambassador for the second edition and brought classmates into it.

The one that got away

The selection for the international team ran training on jobs capped at 15 minutes. Instead of saving checkpoints and stacking progress across jobs, I kept starting from zero, and my model stalled around 70% accuracy when it could have reached about 95%. One missing line of code between me and Beijing. I still think about it.

Rockets from zero

Stack
Solid motors, Arduino, servos, accelerometer
Teacher
BPS.space on YouTube

Planes led to rockets. I started by taking fireworks apart and mounting them in cardboard tubes. The first ones exploded; after enough iterations they flew. From there I learned to mix my own solid propellant, then moved into avionics: an Arduino flight computer reading an accelerometer and driving servos, a parachute deployment system, and cameras on board to film each flight.

No lab, no club, no budget: YouTube and a lot of failed launches.

I noticed my feed kept serving the same repeatable video format with millions of views, so I tried to reverse-engineer it. The first two accounts went nowhere. The third reached 180,000 followers.
